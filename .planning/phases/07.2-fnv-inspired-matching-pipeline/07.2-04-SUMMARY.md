# Phase 07.2 Plan 04: reverseapoc causes Summary

**`reverseapoc causes` reads saved drive evidence (checks.jsonl and checks/NNNN.json) and prints per-try build-failure, mismatch and match rates per run and in total. It also counts Watcom codes and first-Error-line keys through batch's own `compile_output_key`, sorts mismatches into reloc, length, bytes and not-compiled classes, and ranks shared-header gaps by the functions they block.**

- **Duration:** about 20 min (the full host suite alone took 11 min)

## Accomplishments

- A stdlib `causes` verb over `flags._run_dirs` and `flags._attempt_dirs`. Each plain check counts as one try: exit 0 is a match, 1 a mismatch, 2 a build failure. Sweeps are counted apart, malformed lines are counted, and extra keys such as 07.2-03's `note` are ignored. An unknown run exits 2 with a named error.
- `batch.compile_output_key(output)` is the one first-Error-line rule. `_compile_key` delegates to it and `causes` calls it, so the rule exists in one place only.
- Mismatch classes come from the claimed function's saved entry. The order is: `not compiled`, then `batch._reloc_key`, then `length` (checked against the functions.csv row via `stats.read_sizes`), then `bytes` or `bytes: ours/original`, which takes the mnemonics of the window's first row.
- Shared-header gaps are E1011 symbols plus member or subscript errors. They are ranked by distinct (exe, function), with ties broken by name. When nothing is found the output says `shared-header gaps: none`. When build failures are under 1% of plain checks, `format()` adds a note that build failures were few (the RESEARCH Q5 caveat).
