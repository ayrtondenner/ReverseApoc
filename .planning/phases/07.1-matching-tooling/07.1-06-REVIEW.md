## Critical Issues

### CR-01: `cases:` numbers are table indexes, not the `switch`'s case values

**File:** `.claude/skills/watcom-decomp/SKILL.md:131`
**Issue:** The bullet calls the note "the target of each case number" and then says "They are your `switch`'s cases." `asm.case_notes` numbers entries with `enumerate` over the table (`tools/reverseapoc/asm.py:316`), so each number is the position in the table, always from 0. A test pins this: each jump reads its own table "from case 0" (`tests/test_asm.py:374-378`).

The repo's only matched switch disproves the sentence. `func_0001E860` in `src/tacp/u_0001df90.c:36-52` has `case 5`, `case 10`, `case 11` and `case 12`, and its table `D_0001E838` holds 8 entries. So entry 0 is `case 5`, and entries 1 to 4 are not cases at all: they send values 6 to 9 past the cases. A session following the skill writes `case 0:` through `case 7:`, which gives the wrong compare and wrong table contents.

This is common. Over the local listings, 41 of 179 table jumps change the index register within the six instructions before the jump (a rough count that can miss some), and 97 of the 177 jumps with a table listing see a repeated target. That count reads the whole data-in-code row, so a row holding several tables can overstate it. `lift.py`'s draft agrees: it switches on the adjusted index (`lift.py:1427-1428`), so its case numbers are not source values either.
**Fix:**
```markdown
- `cases: 0: .L00034590, 1: .L000345A0` on a table jump: the target of each table entry, by its place in the table, always from 0. The number is not your case value: read the `sub`, `dec` or `add` on the index above the jump, and add that bias back (a first case of 5 makes entry 0 `case 5`). Entries that share the default's target are values with no `case`.
```

## Warnings

### WR-01: The table rule drops "in the same unit"

**File:** `.claude/skills/watcom-decomp/SKILL.md:90`
**Issue:** The first condition says the table's row "sits right before your function's row in `config/<exe>/functions.csv`". `leading_table` only looks among the unit's rows (`check.py:409-414`, with `rows` filtered by unit at `check.py:1172`), and `docs/matching.md:101` says "a switch needs its table row in the same unit". With no such row, `test_no_leading_table_without_its_data_row` shows the jump is refused.

Two real functions meet the skill's three conditions and still cannot pass: `func_0006C290` and `func_00089980` (UFO2P). Each is the first row of its unit, each listing names the table in the row right before it, and that row is in the previous unit (`D_0006C240` in `u_0006b8b0`, `D_0008995C` in `u_00089240`). A session would spend its budget on a function no C can match.
**Fix:** Change the condition to "sits right before your function's row in `config/<exe>/functions.csv`, with the same `unit`". Add: "A table row in another unit cannot pair: stop and say so in notes.md."

### WR-02: Most case targets have no `.L` label in the listing

**File:** `.claude/skills/watcom-decomp/SKILL.md:131`
**Issue:** The bullet calls a target "a `.L` label of the listing". `asm._local_labels` adds labels for direct jumps only (`asm.py:154-162`), and `branch_target` is None for a table jump. A label reached only through the table therefore has no `.LXXXXXXXX:` line. Over the local listings, 2,598 of 2,907 entries (89%) have no such line, counted per jump over its whole data-in-code row, so a row holding several tables counts more than once. A session that searches for the label finds nothing and may conclude that the target is missing.
**Fix:** "`.L00034590` names address `0x00034590`. Most have no label line of their own, so find the line that opens with that address."

### WR-03: "The first declared sits highest" is wrong across blocks

**File:** `.claude/skills/watcom-decomp/SKILL.md:67`
**Issue:** The rule has no block qualifier, but M4 says otherwise: "an earlier block sits lower, the first declared of a block highest" (`probe/README.md:108`, `docs/matching.md:73`). For ints in two blocks, the first declared overall sits lower, the opposite of the skill.

Chars and shorts do not follow their blocks at all: `order_is_known` keeps order across blocks for size 4 only (`slots.py:52-59`, `pr_tie_scope_shorts`). So "swapping two declarations swaps their slots" has no measured support for a 1- or 2-byte frame spread over blocks. `SUMMARY` says the slot rule follows `docs/matching.md`, but this half of that sentence was dropped. GREEN's S1 tested two address-taken ints, which does not exercise blocks.
**Fix:** Add: "That holds inside one block. Across blocks, ints sit by the block's place in the source, an earlier block lower, and chars and shorts do not follow their blocks."

### WR-04: "Only" and "one exception" claim more than was measured

**File:** `.claude/skills/watcom-decomp/SKILL.md:67-68`
**Issue:** Three statements go past the evidence, all from `slots.py:13-26` and `probe/README.md:111-114,124`:
- L67's "moves only address-taken locals and aggregates" leaves out volatiles. A volatile takes a slot with no address taken, and M2 places it (`pr_slot_volatile`).
- L68's "Register-candidate scalars ignore declaration order" with "One exception" is too wide. `slots.py` limits M5 to integers and pointers, and says a caller "must not read M5 as covering locals under 4 bytes". A float with no address taken in a tie still moves with its declarations (`test_declarations_still_move_a_float_in_a_tie`), and a double moves the ints beside it by its use alone (`test_a_double_changes_a_tie_of_ints_by_its_use_alone`).
- L67's "breaks ties only when the frame has one size" reads as "elsewhere declarations do nothing". The evidence says the order there is unknown, not fixed. `order_is_known` refuses those frames, and `test_a_refused_tie_leaves_declaration_order_and_keeps_sizes` shows only that the order is not the declaration order. The `pr_tie_*` shapes have no `_rev` twins, so whether a swap moves them is unmeasured, and for floats it does. A session in a mixed-size frame would drop a move that may work.
**Fix:** Scope L68 to "Integer and pointer register candidates of 4 bytes". Name volatiles beside address-taken locals in L67. Replace the tie sentence's reach with: "In any other tie the order is not known: it is not the declaration order, but a swap may still move it, so one swap is a fair single check."

### WR-05: A call note's registers are not the call's arguments

**File:** `.claude/skills/watcom-decomp/SKILL.md:132`
**Issue:** The bullet says the note shows "what each argument register holds" and that "A register left out is unknown, not zero and not absent". `note_values` keeps any of `eax`, `edx`, `ebx`, `ecx` in `self.needed` (`lift.py:1475-1482`), and that set is liveness, so it includes values read after the call. The list is wrong in both directions:
- **Shown but not an argument.** In `test_call_notes_show_a_value_before_the_call_names_it` (`tests/test_lift.py:1783-1789`) the note shows `ebx=...` on a call to a bare `ret`, only because the caller reads `ebx` later. L40 tells a session to prototype "the argument count the target passes in registers", so such a note invites an extra parameter.
- **Left out because it really is absent.** `test_call_notes_leave_out_a_register_the_lifter_only_named` (`tests/test_lift.py:1800-1806`) leaves `edx` out because "Nothing wrote it and nothing reads it at the call". "Not absent" is false there.
**Fix:** "`eax=<expr> edx=<expr>` on a call: what those registers hold when something still reads them, the call or code after it. A shown register is not proof of an argument, and a missing one is not proof of none: take the argument count from the callee's listing."

### WR-06: `cases:` notes do not come from the converter's symbolic state

**File:** `.claude/skills/watcom-decomp/SKILL.md:129`
**Issue:** The paragraph says "Notes come from the converter's symbolic state, not from the original bytes: read them as hints". That holds for call notes (`lift.call_notes`). `cases:` notes come from `asm.case_notes`, which reads the table's LE fixups directly (`asm.py:290-319`, `attempt.py:1126`) and never runs the lifter. They are facts from the binary, so a session told to doubt them may re-derive what is already exact. "No note means the converter knows nothing there" is wrong for the same reason: a table jump has no note when its table is in no data-in-code row or is malformed (`asm.py:281-287,306-308`).

The wording comes from the plan's Task 2 text, and the code does not support it.
**Fix:** Split the sentence: "`cases:` is read from the original's fixups and is exact. The call notes come from the converter's symbolic state: read them as hints, check them against the listing, and let `attempt check` prove the C."

### WR-07: A session cannot see "every difference", so `slots only` will be guessed

**File:** `.claude/skills/watcom-decomp/SKILL.md:197`
**Issue:** "If every difference you can read is a stack slot, write `slots only`". A session reads one location and one count: `compare_bytes` returns `first_va` and `differing` (`check.py:750-752`), and L177 says it gets no disassembly of its own object. `SUMMARY`'s "Seen, not built" says the same. So one readable difference satisfies the condition even when `differing` is 40 and the rest are not slots. It also pulls against L202's "Every fact in notes.md is read, never guessed". The owner would then search on a false label, because nothing else qualifies it: no tool reads the phrase, which appears only at L197.
**Fix:** "If the original instruction at `first_va` differs only in a stack slot, write `slots only?`, with that instruction and `differing`: you see the first difference and a count, never the rest. The owner runs the search outside sessions, and a session never does."

### WR-08: "If your function cannot come first" is undefined, for most tables

**File:** `.claude/skills/watcom-decomp/SKILL.md:91`
**Issue:** The skill never says when a function cannot come first, and this affects more than half the tables. Of 144 fixup tables, 78 sit in the 33 units that hold two or more. At most one table can lead a unit, and three different situations need different answers:
- A sibling without a table is already C. Your function can go above it, as `SUMMARY` concedes ("an agent that puts its own function first would also pass"). But L99 says to put the definition "after the marker block", and L69's "A function that already matches is never reordered" can read as forbidding the move.
- A sibling with its own leading table is already C. Moving yours first fails that sibling, and L139 needs the whole unit to pass.
- A sibling with a table is still `INCLUDE_ASM`. Yours can lead, and that sibling can then never match. A session should say so in notes.md for the owner.

S2 tested only the simple stop case.
**Fix:** "First means the first definition in the file. You may put yours above a function with no table: L69 is about declarations, and file order does not change that function's verdict, except a fall-through pair (`docs/matching.md`). If a C sibling's table already leads, stop. If a sibling that still has `INCLUDE_ASM` also has a table, go on and note that it can no longer lead."

### WR-09: The relocation list now contradicts the jump-table rule

**File:** `.claude/skills/watcom-decomp/SKILL.md:85`
**Issue:** L81 says check passes a function "only when all of these hold", and L85 lists "no reference to data of the unit's own segments". A leading table is exactly that: data in the unit's own `_TEXT`, referenced by the jump. `_head_reloc` accepts it (`check.py:480-491`). L85 was unchanged and was true while the skill banned jump tables, and the new L90 makes it false as written. A session with a switch reads two rules that disagree.
**Fix:** "no reference to data of the unit's own segments, such as a string literal; the one exception is a leading jump table (see "Strings, statics and jump tables")."

### IN-02: The listing has its own `  ; ` notes

**File:** `.claude/skills/watcom-decomp/SKILL.md:129`
**Issue:** L129 defines a note as what follows `  ; ` and says the listing files do not show notes. `asm._field_operands` writes `  ; <kind> -> <name>` into the listing itself when a fixup does not line up with an operand (`asm.py:213-216`). Those notes do reach the listing files and `attempt context`.
**Fix:** "A `  ; <kind> -> <name>` note is the listing's own, from an LE fixup: it is a fact, and it also shows in the listing files."

### IN-03: `r_p<n>` counts from 5

**File:** `.claude/skills/watcom-decomp/SKILL.md:136`
**Issue:** "`r_p<n>` a stack parameter" leaves out what `<n>` means. The first stack parameter is `r_p5` (`lift.py:901,985`), the argument's position after the four registers. A session could read `r_p5` as a sixth parameter or a fifth stack slot. This line and `s_<hex>` have no scenario: `SUMMARY`'s S5 covers `r_<reg>`, `al=` and `h_<address>`.
**Fix:** "`r_p<n>` is the n-th argument, passed on the stack: `r_p5` is the first."

### IN-05: The `cases:` format example is the rare, refused shape

**File:** `.claude/skills/watcom-decomp/SKILL.md:131`
**Issue:** The example's `1: func_00034700` is a target in another row, which check always refuses. A target inside the row prints as `.L`, so a row name here always means another row (`asm.py:310-311`). Only 4 entries of one real jump have this shape, out of 179 jumps. The bullet also leaves out two forms the code prints: `func_X+0x1` for an address inside another row (`tests/test_asm.py:377`) and a bare address for a target in no row (`test_case_notes_print_a_target_in_no_row_as_its_address`). Neither names a row, though both also lie outside the function. The bullet also does not say what to do on a refused target, though S3's re-run stopped with a note.
**Fix:** Use two `.L` targets in the example, as in CR-01's fix, then: "Anything else (a row name, `func_X+0x1` or a bare address) lands outside your function: check refuses it, so stop and say so in notes.md."
