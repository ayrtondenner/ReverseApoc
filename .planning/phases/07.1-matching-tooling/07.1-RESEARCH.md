## Summary

The tools can be built. Everything they need exists: `batch.compile_batch`, `batch.judge`, the `reverseapoc batch` verb and spike 010's lifter, which still runs unchanged on today's tree. A full dry run of the gate took **122 s on the host** at `172e713f`. Compile cost is not the limit: about 600 compiles plus judging is 6 s per function at 64-255 B, 12 s at 256-1023 B and about 40 s at 1 KB and up.

Three findings should change the planning, and the first needs the owner before plan 1 starts.

1. **Under the plain reading of D-08 the phase stops after plan 2, before the converter exists.** The roadmap makes both numbers converter counts. Plans 1 and 2 do not touch the converter, so both are flat by construction, D-10 fires, and the YAGNI guard "no two flat plans in a row" fails, which reads as HURTS at D-24. Plans 4 and 5 cannot move either number under any reading. Redefining the sample as "through every tool built so far" helps, but measured evidence says plan 1 alone would still move it by 0 or 1 exact row.
2. **Declaration order is the wrong lever for spilled scalars.** On our own clean-room C, reversing declarations gave identical code for scalars that compete for registers, on both flag cells. Slots follow declaration order only for address-taken locals and aggregates. On tacp 0x000A19D0, the target agents named for this tool, 23 declaration orders gave 2 distinct objects and no gain. D-01's type axis does move spills; a statement-order axis, which D-01 lacks, moves them too.
3. **Converter drafts are not near misses, so a search over them has little to find.** Of 298 compiled drafts at 64 B and up, the median best score is 53.85, and masking stack offsets changes the count at 90 and above by nothing. Stack masking does pay on agent C at 1 KB and up (82.75 to 94.05, 50.56 to 76.24), but that C exists for only about 9 rows at 256 B and up.

**Primary recommendation:** Before plan 1, get the owner's ruling on the gate definition (Open Question 1), and record curve point 0 with the `reverseapoc batch` route below. Keep plan 3's work in the order that clears the most refusals: `ebp` as a general register first, because all 66 "stack-relative" refusals are false.

## Plan 1: `reverseapoc search`

### What the harness already gives

- `compile_batch(jobs, workers, *, root=REPO_ROOT, ..., gate=None, ...)` gates once per (compiler, version), then runs a thread pool (batch.py:147-183). One `Job` per object; a duplicate object is refused (`_check_jobs`, lines 99-111).
- `judge(exe, obj, start)` returns `Verdict(exact, reloc_ok, bytes_ok, differing, len_ours, len_row, score, key, exact_trim)`, and `exact` is check's own verdict, 07-48 filler rule included (lines 327-339, 351-379).
- `read_ascii`, `refusal`, `parse_address`, `cells_for`, `cell_record`, `with_source`, `write_cell_jobs`, `judged_record` cover intake and records. `_run_judge` (cli.py:1055) is the verb to copy.
- A search output JSONL built from `with_source` records stays readable by `flags propose --from-batch`.

### What masking stack offsets needs

`_tokens` (batch.py:233-243) keeps `exact_text` for any instruction with no relocated field, so an `[esp + 0x10]` difference counts as a whole-token miss. The change is a mask inside `_tokens` that rewrites the `esp` displacement and the `sub esp, N` immediate. Extend `Verdict` with a defaulted field after `exact_trim`, because `Verdict(...)` is built positionally at lines 362, 365, 375 and 400. `cell_record` picks its keys explicitly, so existing JSONL stays stable.

**Slot-map details the planner must pin with hand-built `Insn` tests:**

- `esp` moves inside a function: pushes for stack arguments shift offsets until the call. Under `__watcall` the callee pops (`ret N`); a stack-convention callee is cleaned with `add esp, N` after the call. The lifter tracks this in `Block_.esp` (lift.py:523, 713-719, 783-784, 909). A linear scan needs the same rule, or the map is wrong after the first pushed argument.
- Aligned pairs share the same delta, so a slot map may be built from aligned instruction pairs.
- Report whether the map is a bijection. A conflict (one of our slots mapping to two of theirs) means slot sharing or a different frame, not a permutation.
- Report the frame sizes too: `sub esp, N | sub esp, N` is the most common first difference on drafts (45 rows at 64 B and up).

### Cost of about 600 compiles

[VERIFIED: measured this session, host, 12 workers]

| Bucket | Judge s/object | 600 judges | 600 compiles at 3.4 ms wall | Per function |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 64-255 | 0.0069 | 4.1 s | 2.0 s | about 6 s |
| 256-1023 | 0.0164 | 9.8 s | 2.0 s | about 12 s |
| >=1024 | 0.0626 | 37.6 s | 2.0 s | about 40 s (few rows measured; worse for 4 KB) |

- **Judging is the bottleneck, and it is single-threaded.** `diff_summary` runs difflib on every object.
- Most variants compile to the same bytes: 3.28 distinct objects per 25 on drafts, 12.5 on spike rows. Hash the function's bytes and judge each distinct body once. That is the largest single saving and costs a few lines inside `search.py`.
- Drop duplicate source text before compiling, as `variants.py:84-86` does.
- Compile in waves of one `compile_batch` call each; a gate costs 0.02 s.
- The container is slower: 0.018 s wall per compile (spike 010: 10.90 s for 600), so about 11 s of compile per function. Run gate-sized searches on the host.
- All 445 rows at 256 B and up: about 2.4 hours on the host, no model cost. Only rows with a draft or an agent C are searchable, which is 87 of them today plus a handful of agent files.

### Where the moves come from, and what they can reach

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

| Address-taken, mixed sizes, arrays | breaks ties only; size decides | - | not tested apart | no effect | size decides | - |
| Scalar competing for registers | **no effect** | **no effect** | **moves** | **moves** | **moves** | **moves** |

**Consequences for the plan:**

- Declaration moves belong on address-taken locals, arrays and structs. Elsewhere they are compiles spent on nothing, though the byte-hash dedupe makes that cheap.
- The type axis is the productive one for spills, so run it first.
- D-01 has no statement-order axis. Assignment and use order are what move spills. Adding one goes beyond the locked scope, so it is Open Question 2. Do not add it without a ruling.
- Text-only moves are enough: a declaration block at the top of the function, one declarator per line. Split multi-declarator lines first. `unitdefs._flatten_braces`, `_split_top` and `declared_names` already exist.
- Always re-run `batch.refusal` on the input, and refuse a FILE holding `#pragma` or inline assembly.

### Preliminary rules (to re-derive as `probe/` sources)

[VERIFIED: measured this session on clean-room C; a preview, not the calibration]

- **M1. Memory locals sort by size.** The largest sits at the lowest offset, the smallest at the highest. `char`, `int`, `short`, `double` gave `double` at 0, `int` at 8, `short` at 0xC, `char` at 0x10, with both declaration orders. This confirms the lead spike 010 recorded ("10.6 sorts frame slots ... by size").
- **M2. Ties go by declaration.** Among equal sizes the first declared takes the highest offset. `a b c d` gave 0xC, 8, 4, 0; `d c b a` gave the reverse; use order changed nothing. Arrays follow the same rule by total size.
- **M3. Sizes pad to 4.** A `short` and a `char` each took a 4-byte slot.
- **M4. Scope changes the order.** Three `int`s in nested blocks reversed against the flat form; the frame stayed 0xC, so slots were not shared.
- **M5. Register-candidate scalars ignore declarations and scope.** Spill slots ascend from 0 by allocator order. Assignment order, use order, type and loop weight change both who is in memory and where.
- Slots were the same across the two cells in the memory-local shapes. Under loop weight (E6, E7) the register picks differ between cells.

The matching.md entry "Declaration order decides stack slots" (docs/matching.md:76, a lead "not yet verified here") is only partly true, and plan 2 should say so.

### What the model can honestly be

- M1-M4 are a small pure function: locals (size, declaration index, scope) to offsets, and its inverse: target offsets to a declaration order.
- M5 is register allocation. Modelling it means reimplementing the allocator's weighting, which spike 010 lists as a lead ("register allocation sorts by loop-weighted savings") and which KKND and NHL95PC also fought. Plan it as a **diagnosis only**: "these slots hold scalars, declaration moves cannot reach them". Do not plan an allocator emulation.
- NHL95PC's `wccre/` and Daggerfall's `w10_frame.py` target 10.0 and 10.0a `-d2` (spike 010 Q1), so they are ideas to compare, not values to copy. D-02 keeps calibration on our own probe regardless.

### How it feeds plans 1 and 3

- **Plan 1:** given the target's slot map, compute the declaration order of memory locals instead of permuting them, and skip declaration moves when the mismatched slots are scalars. Plan 2 therefore edits `search.py`.
- **Plan 3:** `decls += [f"    {t} {n};" for n, t in sorted(self.locals.items())]` (lift.py:506) sorts names as strings, so `s_10` sorts before `s_8`. M2 says declare by ascending distance from entry. That is one line.
- **Limit:** lifted `s_xx` locals are plain scalars, so the compiler may keep them in registers, which is why frame sizes differ on drafts. A model cannot force a local into memory.

### The KeyError

The PRD calls it a jump to filler. **On the measured cases it is not filler.** [VERIFIED: measured this session]

- 2 Tier 1 rows crash, both >=1024: ufo2p 0x00010940 and tacp 0x00010EE0. Their own rows are fine. The crash comes from a callee's signature.
- The crash lands only on the first row to reach the bad callee: `self.sigs[va] = None` is set before the `try` (lift.py:302) and survives the escape, so later callers read `None` and pass. The count therefore depends on row order.
- The targets are 0xC158A and 0xC39C7, **mid-instruction** inside runtime rows: `rt_strncat` and `rt_strstr`. Each lands on the byte after `test ax, 0xcf89`, a jump into a hidden instruction in hand-written library code. One game row does the same: func_000B2040 jumps to 0xB208D, the second byte of a 2-byte `inc ebx`.
- Mechanism: `blocks()` appends a successor at lines 350-351 for any in-row target, but no block starts there, so `live_in[t]` at line 382 raises KeyError. `callee()` catches only `Unliftable, StopIteration, AttributeError, RecursionError` (line 305), and `call_nregs` only `Unliftable` (line 315), so it escapes into the caller.
- Walking all game and thunk rows in address order, `prepare` gives 2,847 ok, 228 unliftable, 4 KeyError (ufo2p 0x00010010, tacp 0x000102A0, 0x00010AF0, 0x000B2040).

**Fix:** in `blocks()`, raise `Unliftable("jump: into an instruction")` when a target has no instruction start. `callee()` then returns `None` for that signature, and the call-site evidence decides. A target that really lies in dropped filler would need remapping, but no such row exists: 0 not-matched Tier 1 rows jump to filler. Do not build remapping.

This fix empties `crash` (2 rows at point 0) and makes the outcome independent of row order.

## Common Pitfalls

### Pitfall 1: a flat curve that is flat by construction
**What goes wrong:** D-10 ends the phase after plan 2. **Why:** the gate measures the converter and plans 1-2 do not touch it. **Avoid:** Open Question 1 before plan 1. **Warning sign:** a plan whose closing task cannot name a mechanism for movement.

### Pitfall 2: permuting what cannot matter
**What goes wrong:** 600 compiles spent on declarations of register-candidate scalars. **Why:** M5. **Avoid:** type moves first, byte-hash dedupe. **Warning sign:** one or two distinct objects from dozens of variants.

### Pitfall 3: `refused` falling while `exact` does not
**What goes wrong:** plan 3 looks like a win. **Why:** spike 010 iterations 1-6. **Avoid:** report outcomes separately, `hole-draft` apart.

### Pitfall 4: a gate that drifts
**What goes wrong:** curve points are not comparable. **Why:** `result-used-*.json` is a gitignored input, `sigs` is shared across rows and order matters on call cycles (lift.py:300-307), and `cflags.txt` changes on landings. **Avoid:** same row order every run, record hashes, pin text equality across the plan 3 move.

### Pitfall 5: judge-exact read as landable
**What goes wrong:** the curve counts rows that cannot land. **Why:** tail merging and sibling regressions in a unit (`land_converter.py:33-36`). **Avoid:** say "judge-exact" in `07.1-CURVE.md`.

### Pitfall 6: an unrequested flag on `search`
**What goes wrong:** `--cell`, `--budget`, `--seed` appear because `judge` has one. **Why:** habit. **Avoid:** constants; the review records `IN-` findings. A module constant for the budget is enough.

### Pitfall 7: the host and container suites together
**What goes wrong:** false failures on shared `build/test-*` directories. **Avoid:** D-14. The full host suite took 974 s at qvp, so budget for it.

### Pitfall 8: game text where it does not belong
**What goes wrong:** a spike 011 agent saved listings to the scratchpad against its brief. **Avoid:** drafts, listings and search output stay under `build/`; plan 5 output stays on the terminal; records hold addresses and shapes only.
