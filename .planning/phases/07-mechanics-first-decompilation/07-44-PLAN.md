This is a gap closure. The owner's first Tier 1 night (2026-10-31, run 20261031-143342) sent ufo2p func_000129F0
(unit u_000126f0) to a session. The row is 3 bytes with no ret: it is not code. Its neighbours look the same:
- func_000126F0 is 768 bytes, all zero;
- func_000129F3 is a short text run followed by zero fill;
- func_00012A33 is a text run plus a long zero run.
All four are Tier 1 must-match members. config/asm-functions.csv lists 0x000129F3 and 0x00012A33 as `unaligned` /
`unclear`, and does not list the other two at all. Only verdict `asm` takes a row out of must-match
(mechanics.`_asm_pairs`), and 16 unmatched Tier 1 members sit on the list as `unclear`. While those rows stay, the 07-39
done condition (`mechanics status --tier 1 --require-matched`) can never hold. Each of them also burns two Opus passes and
then unlimited Fable passes at USD 12.50 each (D-08, D-09, D-25).

The owner ruled on 2026-10-31: "Reclassify in 07-44". The plan lists every must-match row that looks like data, with its
evidence, so the owner can rule on it. The owner sets the verdicts and re-signs [asm_list] and the tiers (F36), and until
then the picker skips those rows.

Decisions taken here, with their reasons:
- **The detector (D-23).** Four new evidence tags, named in must_haves. The thresholds come from a read-only planning
  probe on main (interfaces):
  - no tag fires on any of the 10 matched game rows or on any of the 245 signed asm rows;
  - zero-fill and text-run never fire on library code where they would not belong.

  The detector proposes data rows as `unclear` and never writes `asm` or `c` for them. D-23 says "the tool owns exe,
  address and evidence; verdict and reason are written by hand".
- **What the picker skips.** It skips a row that is verdict `unclear` with at least one data tag on the *signed* list,
  and nothing else. The reasons:
  - **D-23.** The skip keys on the owner having not ruled yet (`unclear`) plus tool-owned evidence. An owner `c` always
    puts the row back in the queue, and only an owner `asm` takes it out of must-match. Skipping every row with data
    evidence, whatever its verdict, would override the owner's `c`.
  - **The signed list only.** Reading the list only through the [asm_list] sign-off check means no hand edit can hide a
    row from the night.
  - **D-09 and D-08.** The skip spends no Opus or Fable pass on a span that cannot compile from C. The rows stay in the
    must-match sets; the driver gets no human step; passes for real functions stay unlimited.
  - **D-02.** It already keeps data-in-code rows out of the call trees, and this is the queue-side interim of that rule.
  - **Signed digests.** The hold changes none. The members file, every tier digest and `mechanics check` are byte for
    byte as they were (tests pin this).
- **`mechanics status` stays honest.** Every total stays the signed must-match count. A separate `held` count and list
  is shown per tier, and `--require-matched` still fails over a held row and names the held count, so a hold can never
  make a tier done (D-05).
- **The verdict vocabulary is unchanged** (asm, c, unclear). A row the owner wants out of must-match because it is data
  gets `asm`, with a reason in the owner's words. D-23's effect is the one wanted: the row stays `INCLUDE_ASM`, leaves
  the queue and the must-match sets, and is drawn outside the percentage. A separate `data` verdict, or re-kinding the
  rows as `data-in-code` in functions.csv (that kind exists, and D-02 excludes it), is a later plan if the owner wants it
  (Task 4 option not-yet).
- **Two owner checkpoints**, because the digests depend on the verdicts:
  - Task 4: the owner writes the verdicts;
  - Task 6: the owner re-signs, after the executor has shown list_sha256, `mechanics digest --tier N` and the must-match
    diff (07-22 / 07-43 style).
